# King and pawn endings

## When to use

Only kings and pawns are left, or you are deciding whether to trade into
such an ending. These positions are pure technique — the rules below decide
them, not inspiration.

## The idea

The king becomes a fighting piece: **the side whose king is more active
usually wins.** One extra pawn promotes if its king escorts it from in
front; promotion converts to a won queen ending
([[mates/king-queen-mate]]).

## What to do

- **King in front of its pawn, pawn behind:** advance the *king* as far as
  possible; push the pawn only when needed for its own safety. King two
  squares in front of the pawn = always winning.
- **The opposition:** kings facing each other with one square between —
  whoever must move gives way. Use a spare pawn move to "lose" a tempo and
  put the opponent on move. When the opponent's king takes a waiting step
  aside, advance your king diagonally, leaving a file free between kings.
- **Defending a pawn down:** keep your king directly in front of the enemy
  pawn; if pushed away, stand directly in front of the enemy *king*.
- **Pawn races: count.** Moves for your pawn to promote vs theirs, including
  any capture-tempo. Equal count → who queens *first*, and does that queen
  cover the other promotion square (a queen on a8 sees h1)?
- **Creating a passed pawn (3 v 3 on one wing):** advance the *centre*
  candidate first (b5+c5+a5 vs a7+b7+c7: 1.b6! axb6 2.c6! bxc6 3.a6 and
  queens). In general, push the pawn that has no enemy pawn opposing it.
- **One pawn can hold two** — fix two enemy pawns with one of yours before
  starting play elsewhere.

## Watch out for

- Pushing the pawn ahead of the king turns a win into a draw (defender
  blockades).
- **Stalemate** when the defending king runs out of squares — see
  [[principles/avoid-stalemate]].
- A rook's pawn whose promotion corner your bishop cannot cover is a draw
  if the enemy king reaches the corner — keep a non-rook pawn when choosing.

## Examples

Win: `8/8/5k2/8/5K2/8/4P3/8 w` — 1.Ke4! Ke6 2.e3! (waiting move wins the
opposition) Kf6 3.Kd5 and the king escorts the pawn in. Draw with the same
material: `8/8/8/8/4k3/8/3KP3/8 w` — Black holds the front squares forever.
Both lines verified in `raw/chess-fundamentals-capablanca.md` (Ex. 5–6).
