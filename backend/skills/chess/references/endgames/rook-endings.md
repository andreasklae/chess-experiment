# Rook endings: behind the passer, bridge to win, Philidor to draw

## When to use
Any ending with rooks and pawn(s) — the most common endgame in practice. Both
when you are converting an extra pawn and when you are defending a pawn down.

## The idea
Rook placement decides these endings, not material count. One rule covers most
positions: **put your rook BEHIND the passed pawn — yours OR theirs**
(Tarrasch rule). Behind your own passer, the rook gains scope as the pawn
advances; behind theirs, it attacks the pawn and never gets pushed away. A
rook IN FRONT of its own pawn blocks it and loses the tempo war.

## What to do
- **Converting (R+P vs R):** cut the enemy king off from the pawn's file with
  your rook, walk your king to escort the pawn. **If the defending king cannot
  reach the queening square, you are winning** (exception: a- and h-pawns draw
  far more often). To finish, know the **Lucena bridge**: your king sits on the
  promotion square, cut off by file; put your rook on the 4th rank, step the
  king out, and when the checks come, block them by "building the bridge" —
  rook interposes on the 4th. Read once, then verify each move with
  `chess__imagine_line`.
- **Defending (R vs R+P):** get your king in front of the pawn and your rook
  to YOUR third rank (**Philidor**): the rook on the 3rd stops the enemy king
  from crossing; the moment the pawn reaches its 6th rank, swing the rook to
  the back rank and check from behind forever — draw. Passive rook in the
  corner loses; checking distance (3+ files away) is what keeps the draw.
- **Rook activity outranks a pawn.** An active rook (behind the passer, or on
  the 7th eating pawns) is usually worth more than passively defending one.

## Watch out for
- Grabbing a wing pawn while the opponent's passer runs — count the race
  first (`endgames/king-pawn-endings` §pawn races).
- Trading rooks converts the position to a KING-AND-PAWN ending — only allow
  it if the resulting K+P ending is WON for you (king in front of the pawn /
  square rule). Check before every rook trade.
- Rook-pawn exception: many "winning" a/h-pawn positions are book draws when
  the defending king reaches the corner.
