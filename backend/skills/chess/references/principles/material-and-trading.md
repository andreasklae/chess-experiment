# Material and trading

## When to use
Any time a trade is offered or available, and when deciding whether to simplify.
A trade is rarely "even" — it always helps one side's plan more.

## The idea (piece values & how they shift)
Rough values: pawn 1, knight ≈ bishop ≈ 3, rook 5, queen 9. But values **shift**
(Capablanca):
- **The rook gains power as pieces come off; the knight loses power.** Rooks and
  queens want an open, emptying board; knights want pieces and pawns to hop
  around.
- The **bishop pair** is worth more in open positions.
- A **passed pawn** is worth more the closer it is to queening and the fewer
  pieces remain ([[positional/pawn-strengths]]).

## What to do (the trading rules — advice, not law)
- **Up material → trade pieces (not pawns).** Every trade magnifies your edge and
  brings the won endgame closer. Keep pawns on (you need them to promote).
- **Down material → avoid trades.** Keep pieces on, keep complications and
  counterplay alive; trading helps the side that's ahead.
- **Cramped → trade pieces** to get room; **more space → avoid trades**
  ([[positional/piece-activity]]).
- **Keep mating material.** This agent mates reliably with K+Q and K+R. So **even
  when only slightly ahead, prefer to keep at least one rook or the queen** rather
  than trade down into a minor-piece endgame — keep the tool that finishes the
  game. Don't trade your last rook/queen for a small material gain if it leaves
  you unable to force mate.
- Trade your **bad** pieces, keep your **good** ones (trade the bad bishop, the
  passive knight).

## Watch out for
- "Winning" material that's immediately recaptured is not a gain — count the
  whole exchange (your tools show the net after recapture).
- Trading queens kills an attack — good when defending, bad when attacking a king.
- Simplifying into a *drawn* endgame throws away a win; check the resulting
  endgame is actually winning before mass trades ([[strategy/convert-advantage]]).

## Examples
You're +2 with queens and rooks on: offer queen and rook trades, decline pawn
trades, reach K+R vs K+few and convert. Counter-example: you're +1 but trading
your last rook leaves K+minor vs K — often not enough to win; keep the rook.
