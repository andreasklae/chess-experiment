# Opening principles

## When to use
The first ~10–15 moves, or any time pieces are still on their starting squares.
Capablanca's first rule: **develop your pieces quickly — get them into play as
fast as you can.**

## The idea (the rules)
Follow these unless a concrete tactic overrides them:

1. **Control the centre.** A pawn on e4/d4 (e5/d5 for Black). Capablanca: no
   violent attack succeeds without controlling ≥2 central squares; the centre
   gives the initiative.
2. **Develop pieces, not pawns.** In the opening, move a piece in preference to a
   pawn whenever you can. Every move should bring a new piece into play.
3. **Knights before bishops.** Develop a knight (f3/c3, f6/c6) before committing
   the bishops — you know where knights belong sooner than bishops.
4. **Don't bring the queen out early.** It gets chased by developing moves,
   losing you tempo; develop minor pieces first.
5. **Don't move the same piece twice** in the opening without a concrete reason —
   each tempo should develop a *new* piece.
6. **Castle early.** Get the king safe and connect the rooks
   ([[positional/king-safety]]).
7. **Few pawn moves.** Beginners overuse a3/h3 (a6/h6); each pawn move is a
   tempo not spent developing, and can create weaknesses.

## What to do
- Each move ask: *does this develop a new piece, fight for the centre, or make
  my king safer?* If not, reconsider.
- Aim for: both knights and both bishops out, castled, rooks connected — *that*
  is "finished developing." Then look for a middlegame plan
  ([[positional/evaluate-position]]).
- Don't grab a pawn in the opening if it costs you development or king safety
  ("don't open lines / chase pawns while uncastled").

## Watch out for
- Exceptions exist (gambits sacrifice a pawn for development; hypermodern
  setups control the centre from afar with a fianchetto) — but follow the rules
  until you have a concrete reason not to.
- Exchanging a bishop for a knight in the opening is usually slightly bad without
  compensation (Capablanca).

## Examples
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 (develops + attacks) Nc6 3.Bb5/Bc4 (knight first, then bishop)
…O-O. New piece every move, centre contested, king safe. Contrast: bringing the
queen to h5 on move 2 — it gets hit by …Nf6/…g6 and you lose time.
