# Prophylaxis, blockade & the seventh rank

## When to use
You have a small edge or a quiet position and want to convert it without letting
the opponent generate counterplay. These are the "deny the opponent / fix the
target" skills.

## The idea
- **Prophylaxis** — before making your own plan, ask **"what does my opponent want
  to do?"** and prevent it. A move that stops their freeing break, their only
  active piece, or their counterplay is often worth more than a move that advances
  your own plan. Restrain first, then act.
- **Blockade** — park a piece directly **in front of an enemy passed (or isolated)
  pawn.** It does two jobs at once: the pawn can never advance, and the piece sits
  on a square no enemy pawn can attack (the pawn it blocks shields it). A **knight**
  is the ideal blockader (it still controls squares around it while parked).
- **Seventh rank** — a rook on the enemy's 2nd rank (your 7th) attacks the pawns on
  their starting squares and hems the king; **two rooks (or rook+queen) on the 7th
  ("pigs on the 7th") are often decisive.** A great reason to seize an open file is
  to invade the 7th.
- **Fix a weakness** — make the enemy weakness *permanent* (stop the pawn that
  would trade it away), then pile on it. A fixed target plus an outpost in front of
  it is a winning structure (Lasker, Capablanca).

## What to do
- Each move in a quiet position, **spend one thought on the opponent's plan** and
  whether a prophylactic move kills it more cheaply than pushing your own.
- Against a passed/isolated pawn: **blockade the square in front with a knight**,
  then bring up attackers to win the fixed pawn ([[positional/pawn-weaknesses]]).
- When you win an open file, **drive a rook to the 7th**; double rooks there if you
  can.
- Before trading, ask whether the trade *fixes* an enemy weakness for you or
  *releases* one — keep their structure rigid and bad.

## Watch out for
- Don't get so absorbed in prevention that you never make progress — prophylaxis
  serves a plan, it isn't a plan by itself.
- A blockading piece is doing a job; trading it off lets the pawn run. Keep the
  blockader unless you have something better.

## Examples
Enemy isolated d-pawn → knight to d5/d6 *blockades* it and dominates; rooks come to
the d-file; the pawn is fixed and falls. Open c-file → Rc7 hits the 2nd-rank pawns
and the king can't breathe. See [[positional/piece-activity]] for outposts and
[[positional/pawn-strengths]] for the passer you're blockading.
