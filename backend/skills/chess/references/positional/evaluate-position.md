# Evaluate the position — both sides

## When to use
No tactic is forcing your move and you're not sure what to play for. Before
choosing a move, **assess the position for BOTH colours.** Most mistakes come
from playing without knowing what the position needs.

## The idea
Run the same checklist for **you and the opponent**, then play to your strengths
and their weaknesses (and fix your weaknesses / deny their strengths). Five
things, in priority:

1. **King safety** — is either king exposed? (Dominates everything — see
   [[positional/king-safety]].)
2. **Material** — who's ahead, and by how much?
3. **Pawn structure** — weaknesses ([[positional/pawn-weaknesses]]: isolated,
   doubled, backward, holes, hanging) and strengths
   ([[positional/pawn-strengths]]: passed, connected, majority, chains) for both.
4. **Piece activity** — open files, outposts, good/bad bishops, the bishop pair,
   space ([[positional/piece-activity]]). Who has the more active army?
5. **Initiative** — who is making threats and who is reacting? (Capablanca: keep
   the initiative; only give it up for something concrete.)

## What to do
- **Turn each item into a target or a plan.** Their backward pawn → attack it.
  Your knight has an outpost → put it there. Their king is open → open lines.
  Your bad bishop → trade it. You have a majority → make a passed pawn.
- **Match the plan to the advantage you have:**
  - Material up → trade pieces, simplify, keep mating material (R/Q) — see
    [[strategy/convert-advantage]].
  - Space → manoeuvre, switch wings, *don't* trade.
  - Better structure → blockade and attack the weakness, win it in the endgame.
  - Safer king → attack theirs.
  - Nothing obvious → **improve your worst-placed piece**, or king safety.
- Then commit it to a written plan — [[strategy/make-a-plan]].

## Watch out for
- Don't evaluate only your own side — half the plan is denying the opponent
  theirs. Always ask "what does *my opponent* want?"
- A long-term plus is worthless if you're getting mated or hanging pieces next
  move. Survive the concrete first ([[strategy/handle-a-threat]]), then plan.

## Examples
You: bishop pair + half-open c-file. Them: backward c6 pawn + bad bishop. Plan:
open the position for the bishops, pile rooks on c-file, blockade c5 with a
knight, win the c-pawn. One assessment → a whole game's plan.
