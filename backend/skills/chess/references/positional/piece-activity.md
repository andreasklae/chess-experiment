# Piece activity — where pieces belong

## When to use
The position is quiet and you're improving your army, or deciding which piece to
trade. The guiding question: **is each piece doing something, and can I make my
worst piece better?**

## The idea
- **Open file** (no pawns either side) / **half-open file** (no friendly pawns):
  the highway for **rooks**. Whoever controls the only open file often controls
  the game.
- **Outpost**: a square no enemy pawn can attack (ideally defended by your pawn),
  usually rank 4–6. A **knight** planted there can't be kicked and dominates —
  often worth more than a bishop.
- **Good vs bad bishop**: a bishop is *bad* when your own pawns sit on its colour
  (they block it), *good* when your pawns are on the other colour.
- **Bishop pair**: both bishops vs not; they cover both colours, worth ~half a
  pawn, more in **open** positions.
- **Space**: pawns further advanced → more room. Space cramps the enemy.
- **Color complex / fianchetto**: dominating one colour's squares; a fianchettoed
  bishop guards its long diagonal and the castled king.

## What to do
- **Rooks → open files**; if a rook can reach an open/half-open file, that move
  is often the plan. Double rooks to force entry.
- **Knights → outposts**; manoeuvre a knight toward a hole in the enemy camp.
- **Keep your pawns off your good bishop's colour**; trade away your *bad*
  bishop. Fix enemy pawns on the colour of *their* bishop to make it bad.
- **Bishop pair → open the position** (open lines/diagonals). Without it, keep
  things **closed** and find a knight outpost.
- **Space → don't trade** (you have room, they don't); manoeuvre and switch
  wings. **Cramped → trade pieces** to breathe, and strike the pawn chain to
  open lines.
- **Nothing else to do?** Improve your worst-placed piece. That is always a plan.

## Watch out for
- An extra piece that sits passive is worth less than an active enemy piece —
  activity first.
- Don't trade a fianchetto bishop guarding your king without good reason — it
  wrecks that colour complex around the king.

## Examples
Knight to d5 outpost (no black c- or e-pawn can hit it), defended by your e4
pawn → it eyes f6/b6/c7 and can't be dislodged. See [[positional/pawn-weaknesses]]
for the holes that create outposts.
