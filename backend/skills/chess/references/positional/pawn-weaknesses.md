# Pawn weaknesses

## When to use
The position is quiet and you're deciding what to play for, or `show_position`
flagged a weak pawn/square (yours or theirs). **Structural pawn weaknesses are
usually permanent** — once created they don't go away, so they decide long-term
plans (Capablanca).

## The idea
A weak pawn is one **no friendly pawn can ever defend**, so it becomes a fixed
target. A weakness only matters if you can actually attack it.

- **Isolated pawn** — no friendly pawn on either adjacent file. The square in
  front is a hole the enemy blockades. (An isolated *queen's* pawn, IQP, also
  gives space and attacking chances — a double-edged case.)
- **Doubled pawns** — two pawns on one file; can't defend each other, the front
  one is hard to guard.
- **Backward pawn** — lags behind its neighbours, its advance square controlled
  by an enemy pawn; nasty on a half-open file.
- **Hole / weak square** — a square your pawns can no longer control (the pawns
  that guarded it advanced or were traded). An enemy knight there is untouchable.
- **Hanging pawns** — two pawns abreast with no pawns on the files beside them;
  strong while abreast, weak once forced to advance.

## What to do
**If it's the ENEMY's weakness (attack it):**
- **Blockade** the square in front with a piece (a knight is ideal) — it both
  stops the pawn and sits safely.
- Pile pieces on the pawn down the **(half-)open file**; double rooks.
- **Trade pieces** — the fewer pieces, the more a static weakness tells. Win it
  in the endgame.

**If it's YOUR weakness (hold it):**
- Defend it with pieces; seek active piece play / a freeing pawn break.
- For an IQP or hanging pawns, use the space and open files for an attack
  *before* the weakness becomes the whole story; avoid mass trades.

## Watch out for
- Don't fixate on a weakness you can't reach — if no piece can attack it, it's
  cosmetic.
- Creating a weakness to win one is a bad trade unless you get real piece activity.
- A blockading knight in front of an isolated/passed pawn is doing two jobs —
  don't trade it off carelessly.

## Examples
Backward c-pawn on a half-open c-file → put both rooks on c1/c2 and the knight
on c5 (a hole) → the pawn falls or is permanently tied down. See
[[positional/piece-activity]] for outposts and [[positional/pawn-strengths]] for
the passed-pawn flip side.
