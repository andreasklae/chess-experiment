# London vs the King's Indian setup (...g6, ...Bg7)

## When to use
Black fianchettoes: **...Nf6, ...g6, ...Bg7, ...d6, ...O-O**, aiming for a King's
Indian-style break with **...e5** or **...c5**. The London handles this comfortably —
but adjust two things from the standard setup.

## The idea
Black's plan is to hit your centre with ...e5 (or ...c5) and to trade your good
f4-bishop with **...Nh5**. You keep the centre solid, deny ...e5 a good moment, and
preserve the bishop.

## What to do
- **Put the light bishop on e2, NOT d3.** Against the ...g6 wall a bishop on d3 has
  little scope; **Be2** is more useful (supports the centre, eyes h5/g4). Standard
  setup here: **d4, Bf4, e3, Nf3, Be2, h3, c3, Nbd2, O-O**.
- **Play h3 early** — it gives the f4-bishop the h2 retreat (so ...Nh5 cannot win the
  bishop) and prevents ...Bg4 / ...Ng4 ideas.
- **Meet ...e5** by capturing or holding: usually keep the tension; if Black plays
  ...e5 hitting f4, retreat the bishop to **h2** (after h3) where it still rakes the
  long diagonal and eyes e5. Do not let the bishop get trapped.
- **Meet ...c5** with **c3** (support d4) — same as the main London; if Black takes
  ...cxd4, recapture exd4 and you have a fine centre.
- After the setup, look for **Ne5** and kingside play as usual; Black's king on g8 is
  the target.

## Watch out for
- **...Nh5 to trade your dark bishop** is the key Black resource here — handle it on
  its own page: `openings/london-vs-nh5` (short answer: **Bh2** after you have played
  h3, or **Bg5**; never just lose the bishop).
- **Don't allow a free ...e5** that gains time on the f4-bishop AND grabs the centre —
  have h3 in first so the bishop has h2.
- A **Bd3** here often does nothing useful; prefer Be2.

## Example (verified)
`1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 g6 3.e3 Bg7 4.Nf3 O-O 5.Be2 d6 6.h3 c5 7.c3` →
`rnbq1rk1/pp2ppbp/3p1np1/2p5/3P1B2/2P1PN1P/PP2BPP1/RN1QK2R b` — solid London structure
with Be2 and h3 in, ready for Nbd2 and Ne5.
