# London: the Ne5 outpost and kingside attack

## When to use
You have finished the London setup (d4-e3-c3, Bf4/g3, Nf3, Bd3, Nbd2, O-O) and Black
has castled kingside. This page is **one of the two main plans** — the **kingside
attack**. (The other is the central e3–e4 break: `openings/london-central-break`,
which also explains which pawn structure calls for which plan.) The whole London
points here: a knight on **e5** and an attack on the king.

## The idea
**Ne5 is the heart of the London.** A knight on e5 is supported by the d4-pawn and the
f4/g3-bishop, it can't easily be chased, and it eyes f7/g6/d7 — it makes the "boring"
London genuinely dangerous. From the outpost you build a kingside attack.

## What to do — the attacking recipe (in order)
1. **Play Ne5** when it is well-timed (Black can't immediately hit it with ...Nd7/...f6
   or ...Ne4 favourably). If Black takes it, recapture **dxe5** — this kicks the f6-knight
   away and OPENS the attack (it removes Black's best kingside defender; note this is
   what enables the Greek gift — see `openings/london-bxh7-greek-gift`).
2. **Support e5 with f2–f4** (and later the second knight Nd2–f3 if the first traded).
3. **Bring the queen to the kingside: Qf3 or Qh5** (Qh5 eyes h7; Qf3 supports e4/g4 and
   swings to h3).
4. **Lift a rook: Rf1–f3–h3** (or Rf3–g3) to pile on the king.
5. **Roll the h-pawn: h4–h5**, to open the h-file or provoke ...h6 (then g4–g5). Pushing
   the h-pawn also supports Ng5.
6. **Look for Bxh7+** the moment the Greek-gift pattern appears (no Black Nf6, Bd3 on
   the diagonal, Nf3→g5, queen to h5) — it is the typical breakthrough, not an
   afterthought. Read `openings/london-bxh7-greek-gift`.

## Watch out for
- **Don't play Ne5 too early** — if Black can answer ...Ne4 or ...Nd7 hitting it, or
  ...f6 after you can't recapture dxe5 favourably, wait and improve first.
- **Keep the dark bishop** (f4/g3/h2) — it guards e5 and rakes the kingside; don't let
  ...Nh5 trade it off cheaply (see `openings/london-vs-nh5`).
- This plan is for a **safe own king** — castle first; don't launch the h-pawn if it
  exposes your own king with pieces still undeveloped.

## Model games (verified move orders)
- **Kamsky–Shankland 2014:** 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 d5 3.e3 e6 4.Nd2 c5 5.c3 Nc6 6.Ngf3 Bd6
  7.Bg3 O-O 8.Bd3 Qe7 **9.Ne5** — the outpost, then a Greek-gift breakthrough (Bxh7+
  Kxh7 Qh5+ Kg8 Ne4).
- **Blatny–Luchan 2001:** the same **Ne5** plan (…8.Bd3 Re8 9.Ne5) then **f4, Rf3–h4,
  Bxf6, Qh5** — a model kingside squeeze that ends in a mating attack.
