# London: the central e4 break and the key structures

## When to use
You've completed the London setup and want a plan, but a direct kingside attack
(see `openings/london-ne5-attack`) isn't ripe — Black is solid on the kingside, or
the centre is the natural battlefield. The London has a **second main plan**: break
with **e3–e4** in the centre. Which plan you choose is decided by the **pawn
structure**, so read that part too.

## The idea — e3–e4
Complete development with the queen on **e2** and a rook on **e1** *behind* the
e-pawn, then play **e4**. It gains space and opens lines for your bishops. The break
is especially strong when **Black's bishop sits on d6 opposite your Bg3**: then
**e4** threatens **e5** forking/winning the d6-bishop, and if Black plays **...Bxg3**
to stop it, **hxg3** opens the **h-file** straight at Black's king — both replies help
you (models: Kamsky–Goganov 2016, Carlsen–Kramnik 2019).

## First: if Black just took on d4 (…cxd4), RECAPTURE
Taking back keeps your strong centre — this is theory, not a moment to develop instead.
- **…cxd4 with only the e-pawn able to retake → exd4.** Opens the e-file; you get the
  **Carlsbad** structure below (knight later to d3).
- **…cxd4 when BOTH e- and c-pawns can retake → choose by structure:** **exd4** for the
  e-file and the Carlsbad plan; **cxd4** to keep the c-file and a broad d4/e-pawn centre.
- Only skip the recapture for a bigger forcing move (a check, or a capture winning more),
  or if the recapture itself hangs to a tactic — then calculate.

## What to do — read the structure, then pick the plan
- **Symmetric / Black solid on the kingside → central e4 break.** Qe2, Re1, then e4.
- **Black castled and loose → kingside attack** instead (`openings/london-ne5-attack`).
- **Carlsbad structure (after ...cxd4, exd4):** your knight's ideal home is **d3** —
  it guards b2 and eyes e5. From there, attack with **h4–h5** to open the h-file.
  (Carlsen–Kramnik 2019.) If ALL the minor pieces come off, Black gets ...a5–b4
  queenside play — keep pieces on if you want to attack.
- **Doubled f-pawns (after your Bf4 is traded and you recapture exf4):** this
  actually *strengthens* the **e5** outpost and gives your knights **d4 and e5**.
  But the d4-pawn can become isolated after ...cxd5 — watch it. Plan **Nb3–d4** then
  **f4–f5** (Gukesh–Kjartansson 2020).

## Watch out for
- **Don't push e4 before you're ready** — put the queen on e2 and the rook on e1
  first; a premature e4 just drops the pawn or opens lines for Black.
- After e4–e5 hits the d6-bishop, calculate ...Bxg3 hxg3 — it's good for you (open
  h-file), but make sure you're not missing a Black in-between move.
- In the doubled-f-pawn structure, the **d4-pawn is the weakness** — don't let it
  fall; support it (Nb3–d4, Re1–e-file pressure).

## Examples (verified)
- Ideal deployment then break: `1.d4 d5 2.Bf4 Nf6 3.e3 c5 4.c3 e6 5.Nd2 Nc6 6.Ngf3
  Bd6 7.Bg3 O-O 8.Bd3 b6 9.Qe2 Bb7 10.e4` — fully developed, e4 achieved.
- The Bd6-vs-Bg3 point: `...Bxg3 hxg3` opens the h-file for your rook/queen.
