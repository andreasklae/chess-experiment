# London: meeting an early ...c5 on d4

## When to use
Black plays **...c5** early (e.g. 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 c5, or with ...d5 and a quick ...c5),
hitting your **d4-pawn** before you've finished the **c3** triangle — often paired
with **...Qb6** on b2. You need to hold the centre without panicking.

## The idea
Three sound ways to meet it: (1) **support d4** with c3 and play a normal London;
(2) **capture dxc5** and try to hold the extra pawn / gambit it back for development;
(3) **push d5**, gaining space and steering into a **Benoni-type** structure where
your extra centre space is an asset. Pick by what Black commits to.

## What to do
- **Standard: c3 + e3, build the triangle**, then develop and aim for Ne5/e4. Only
  works when ...Qb6 isn't already winning b2 — check move order (`openings/london-vs-qb6`).
- **The Nc3–Nb5 gambit (…c5 before your c3):** 1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 c5 3.e3 **Qb6 4.Nc3!**
  — if **...Qxb2 5.Nb5!** the knight hits c7 and a7 and the queen is offside; you get
  a big lead in development. (Black usually avoids ...Qxb2 and plays ...d6/...a6 to
  stop Nb5.)
- **Push d5 into a Benoni:** after ...c5 and some trades, **d4–d5** grabs space; e.g.
  `2.Bf4 c5 3.e3 Qb6 4.Nc3 d6 5.Bb5+ Bd7 6.a4 a6 7.a5 Qc7 8.Bxd7+ Nbxd7 9.d5` — a
  comfortable space edge. Your dark bishop on f4 is well placed for these structures.

## Watch out for
- **Don't leave b2 hanging** — if ...Qb6 is on and you can't justify the gambit, defend
  with **b3 / Qc1** (Qb3 only if c3 is already in). See `openings/london-vs-qb6`.
- **The Nb5 gambit is a choice, not a must** — only enter it if you want a sharp game
  and have checked the line; otherwise support d4 quietly.
- After **d5**, don't let Black break with ...e6/...b5 for free — meet pawn breaks with
  developed pieces, keep the space.

## Examples (verified)
- Nb5 gambit: `1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 c5 3.e3 Qb6 4.Nc3 Qxb2 5.Nb5` — knight hits c7/a7, queen
  offside; big development lead.
- d5 into Benoni: `1.d4 Nf6 2.Bf4 c5 3.e3 Qb6 4.Nc3 d6 5.Bb5+ Bd7 6.a4 a6 7.a5 Qc7
  8.Bxd7+ Nbxd7 9.d5` — space advantage, London bishop well placed.
