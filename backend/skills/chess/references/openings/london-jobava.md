# The Jobava London (a sharper sibling)

## When to use
This is a **variant**, not the main repertoire: **1.d4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Bf4** (knight to
c3 instead of the quiet c3-pawn). Use it only when you deliberately want a sharp,
attacking game — otherwise stick to the standard London (`openings/london-system`).
It is flagged here so you **recognise** it and don't confuse its plans with the quiet
London's.

## The idea
By putting the knight on **c3** (not blocking it with the c-pawn), White prepares
**Nb5** (hitting c7/d6) and fast pawn storms **h4–h5** or **g4**, often with
**queenside castling** — a completely different, more violent plan than the calm
Ne5/e4 London.

## What to do
- **vs ...g6:** 1.d4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Bf4 g6 4.e3 Bg7 **5.h4!?** and 6.h5 — go straight
  for the king.
- **vs ...a6 / ...e6:** 4.e3 e6 **5.g4!?** grabbing space and attacking.
- **vs ...c5:** 3...c5 4.e3 cxd4 5.exd4 a6 (stopping Nb5) 6.Nf3 Nc6 — a normal, roughly
  balanced middlegame.

## Watch out for
- **It is NOT the quiet London** — the c-pawn stays home, so d4 is less solidly
  supported and b2 is guarded by the c3-knight, not a pawn. Different weaknesses.
- **Only play it if you want the sharp game** and have looked at the line; a
  half-hearted Jobava (starting the attack, then playing quietly) just loses time.
- With the knight on c3, the ...Qb6 hit on b2 works differently — the knight covers
  the a4–e8 ideas; calculate, don't assume the standard answers apply.

## Examples (verified)
- vs ...c5: `1.d4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Bf4 c5 4.e3 cxd4 5.exd4 a6 6.Nf3 Nc6` — balanced.
- vs ...g6: `1.d4 Nf6 2.Nc3 d5 3.Bf4 g6 4.e3 Bg7 5.h4` — immediate kingside storm.
