# London: meeting ...Nh5 (Black tries to trade your f4-bishop)

## When to use
Black plays **...Nh5**, attacking your **dark-squared bishop** (on f4 or g3). That
bishop is one of the London's best pieces — it controls e5 and rakes toward the
kingside. Black wants to swap it off. Don't let it go for free; pick the right
response for the position.

## The idea
You have three good ways to keep or favourably trade the bishop. Which one depends on
whether you have **h3** in and where Black's pieces are.

## What to do
- **Bishop on f4, you have NOT played h3 → retreat to g5.** **Bg5** keeps the bishop
  active and pins/eyes Black's kingside. (Avoid this if Black has a queen+bishop battery
  aimed at g5; then prefer the h3/Bh2 plan.)
- **You have played h3 (or play it now) → retreat to h2.** **Bh2** keeps the bishop on
  the long b8–h2 diagonal, still controlling e5, completely safe. This is the standard
  King's-Indian-setup answer (you should have h3 in there already).
- **Let Black take it: ...Nxg3 (when bishop is on g3) → recapture hxg3.** **hxg3 opens
  the h-file for your rook** — a real attacking asset once Black has castled kingside
  (Rh1 + queen down the h-file). Trading the bishop is FINE when you get the open h-file
  in return.

## Watch out for
- **Do NOT leave the bishop hanging** — ...Nxf4 winning a clean bishop is a real blunder
  if you forget it is attacked. When you see ...Nh5, address the bishop THAT move.
- A knight on **h5** is offside — after you tuck the bishop away (Bh2/Bg5), the h5-knight
  often has no good square; you can gain time later (g4 ideas, or it must return to f6).
- **Bg5** can run into ...f6 or ...h6 kicking it; have a follow-up (Bh4, or Bxf6/Be3) in
  mind, and don't go to g5 if it just gets chased with tempo.

## Example (verified)
With the bishop on g3 and Black's knight on h5: **...Nxg3 hxg3** opens the h-file for
the h1-rook — a healthy trade. With h3 already played, **Bh2** keeps the bishop safe on
the diagonal.
