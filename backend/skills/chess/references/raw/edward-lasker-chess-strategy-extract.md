# Edward Lasker — *Chess Strategy* (1915): principles digest

> **This is a paraphrased digest, not the source.** The verbatim public-domain
> full text is in `edward-lasker-chess-strategy-GUTENBERG-5614.txt` (Project
> Gutenberg #5614). This file is a convenience summary of the load-bearing
> maxims for wiki ingestion. See `SOURCES.md`.

A second public-domain instructional spine alongside Capablanca. Principles
paraphrased/quoted here for the wiki; Lasker's value is the crisp maxims on
mobility, pawn permanence, and combinations.

---

## Development & the opening
- **Quick development is of the utmost importance**; control the centre.
- Avoid **"loss of a move"** — play only pawn moves that are necessary to develop
  pieces or control the board.
- Centre doctrine: **"Placing the pieces in the centre is of value, because there
  they have more mobility than near the edge."** Control of e4/d4 (and the squares
  around them) lets you concentrate pieces fast.
- First-move advantage: "White, having the first move, is, so to speak, always
  morally justified in attacking, whilst Black should assume the defensive."

## Piece value = mobility
- **"The relative value of the pieces can only make itself felt by their greater
  or lesser mobility"** — mobility is the highest criterion of a piece's worth.
- Standard values (minor = minor; rook = minor + pawns; queen = two rooks) "come
  to nought when the pieces are hindered in their mobility."
- **"Loss of material must be avoided, even if only a pawn. It is a good habit to
  look upon every pawn as a prospective Queen."**

## Position play
- **Weak pawns** (backward, unable to advance with pawn support) are targets and
  force passive, mobility-killing defence.
- **Open files** (opened by pawn exchanges) are highways for rooks; controlling
  the K-/Q-file creates attacking chances.
- **Seventh rank**: advanced pawns/pieces on the 7th create mating threats.
- **Outposts**: "fixing a weakness" by controlling a key square forces the
  opponent passive; a **knight posted on a strong central square gains
  disproportionate strength.**
- **Bishop pair**: "Two Bishops are, generally speaking, of greater value than two
  Knights, because together they also act on all the squares, and their command
  of long diagonals is a clear advantage" — but only in **open** positions where
  they keep mobility.

## Combinations & sacrifices
- **Counting an exchange on one square is elementary arithmetic**: count the
  attackers and defenders of the square and compare their values, in order. (This
  is the static-exchange logic the agent's SEE gate already does.)
- **"Victory is often brought about by a timely sacrifice of material. But in such
  cases the sacrificing of material has its compensation in some particular
  advantage of position"** — a sacrifice must buy a concrete gain (open file, weak
  king, dominant piece), never speculation alone.

## Maxims worth quoting
- **Pawn skeleton is permanent:** "Pawn formation is of a more permanent character
  than that of the pieces... Once a pawn has moved it cannot turn back, and only
  after the greatest deliberation should we embark on changes." (Reinforces:
  pawn weaknesses are forever — think before every pawn move.)
- **Strategy must respect tactics:** "Sound strategy, when setting the task, must
  never lose sight of tactical practicability, and only a thorough knowledge of
  tactical resources makes correct strategy possible."
- **Don't over-calculate what logic settles:** even strong players "lose valuable
  time in thinking out endless variations, to maintain positions which could be
  proved valueless by general and logical deductions."
