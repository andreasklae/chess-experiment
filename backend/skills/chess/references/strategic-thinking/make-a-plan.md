# Make a plan — and keep it

## When to use

The position is quiet and no tactic is on; or you notice your last moves
have been aimless (shuffling, repeating, reacting). Also read this when your
*standing plan* (shown in your memory each turn) no longer matches the board.

## The idea

A plan is a **goal plus a method**, stated in one or two sentences:
"Promote the a-pawn: trade queens if offered, march King to b6, push a4–a8."
Chess is won by many moves pulling in one direction. Because you only see
the game one turn at a time, **a plan you do not write down does not exist**
— write it in the `plan` field when you commit your move, and it will be
shown back to you every turn until you replace it.

## What to do

- **Derive the goal from the position:** ahead in material → convert
  ([[strategic-thinking/convert-advantage]]); enemy king exposed → attack
  it; bad piece → improve it; nothing obvious → improve your worst piece or
  king safety.
- **State the method concretely** — name the pawn, the file, the target
  square. "Play better" is not a plan; "double rooks on the d-file" is.
- **Each turn, ask first: does my standing plan still apply?** If yes, pick
  the candidate move that advances it — only deviate for tactics (a free
  capture, a mate, a threat that must be answered).
- **Replace the plan when:** it is completed, its target vanished (piece
  traded, file closed), or the opponent's last move creates a bigger
  problem/opportunity. Then write the *new* plan when you commit.

## Watch out for

- Re-deriving a new plan every turn is the same as having none. Trust the
  written plan unless the board contradicts it.
- A plan does not excuse you from checking the opponent's threat each turn
  — survive first, then advance the plan.
- Keep it to 1–2 sentences. A plan too long to re-read is a plan you will
  ignore.
