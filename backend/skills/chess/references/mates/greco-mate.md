# Greco's mate

## When to use

The enemy king is castled short, the h-file is open (or openable — the
h-pawn gone or about to be dragged away), your light-square bishop aims at
the a2–g8 diagonal, and your queen can reach the h-file. One of the most
common attacking mates against a castled king.

## The idea

Two lines crossing at the king: the **bishop** controls g8 from afar (the
c4–f7–g8 diagonal), so the king on h8 cannot step back inboard; the king's
own g7-pawn blocks the other flight. The **queen** then owns the entire
h-file — check on it is mate. Neither piece could do it alone; each seals
the other's leak.

## What to do

1. Point the bishop at g8 (c4/b3 with f7 weak or gone).
2. Open the h-file: trade or deflect the h-pawn — a piece sacrifice on g6
   or h7 that forces ...hxg6 (or drags the king out) is the classic method;
   count material against the forced mate first.
3. Queen to the h-file (h5/h4/h3) with check or unanswerable threat — mate
   on h7/h8.

## Watch out for

- The bishop's diagonal must actually reach g8 — a pawn on e6/d5/f7 in the
  way kills the pattern.
- After the sacrifice, the king sometimes runs via f8/e7 — before
  sacrificing, check the inner escape squares are covered or blocked.
- A defender able to interpose on the h-file (...Qh4, ...Nh5) turns mate
  into a trade — scan the file's defenders first.

## Examples

`7k/6p1/8/7Q/2B5/8/8/8 b - - 0 1` — final picture: Qh5 mates on the h-file;
Bc4 covers g8; Black's own g7-pawn blocks the last square. Verified.
