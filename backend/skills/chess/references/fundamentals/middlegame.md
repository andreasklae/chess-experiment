# Middlegame fundamentals

**Goal of the middlegame:** turn your development into something concrete — a
target, an attack, or a better structure — without blundering or letting the
opponent attack first. This is where you need a **plan**.

## Every move
1. **Answer the opponent's threat first** ([[fundamentals/every-move-checklist]]).
2. **Look for a tactic** — checks, captures, threats; forks/pins/discoveries; a
   loose enemy piece ([[tactics/index]]). Tactics flow from a good position.
3. If nothing forcing, **follow or make a plan** (below).

## Making the plan (assess, then target)
Run the position assessment ([[positional/evaluate-position]]) for **both sides**
and pick a target:
- **Enemy king exposed?** → attack it: open lines toward it, bring pieces, don't
  trade your attackers ([[positional/king-safety]]).
- **A weakness in their camp** (backward/isolated pawn, a hole)? → fix it,
  blockade it, pile up and win it ([[positional/pawn-weaknesses]],
  [[positional/prophylaxis-and-blockade]]).
- **A structural plus of yours** (passed pawn, majority, bishop pair, open file)?
  → use it: push the passer, open the position for the bishops, seize the file
  with a rook ([[positional/pawn-strengths]], [[positional/piece-activity]]).
- **Nothing obvious?** → improve your worst-placed piece; put rooks on open files
  and knights on outposts; gain space.

## Keep your pieces working
- **Active pieces beat material that does nothing** — a passive extra piece is
  worth less than an active enemy one.
- **Coordinate**: aim several pieces at the same target (Capablanca: pieces act
  *en masse*). Doubled rooks, a battery, pieces converging on a weak square.
- **Prophylaxis**: before pushing your plan, ask *what does my opponent want?* and
  stop it if it's cheap to ([[positional/prophylaxis-and-blockade]]).

## Trading in the middlegame
- **Up material → trade pieces**, head for a won endgame, keep a rook/queen.
- **Attacking the king → don't trade** the attackers; trading queens kills an
  attack (good when you're defending).
- Trade your **bad** pieces; keep your **good** ones. ([[principles/material-and-trading]])

## When to head for the endgame
If you have a clear structural/material plus and no attack, **simplify** into an
endgame where it converts ([[strategy/convert-advantage]], [[fundamentals/endgame]]).

**One line:** *assess both sides, attack the king or the weakness, keep your
pieces active and safe, and don't drift — a mediocre plan beats no plan.*
