# Endgame fundamentals

**Goal of the endgame:** convert your advantage into a queen or a mate. The rules
change once the queens and most pieces are gone — the king becomes a **fighting
piece**, and pawns become precious (every pawn is a future queen — Lasker).

## Do
- **Activate your king.** In the endgame the king marches up the board to support
  pawns and attack the enemy's — Capablanca: its handling is paramount here. (In
  the middlegame the king hides; in the endgame it fights.)
- **Push passed pawns** and make new ones from a majority; the KING escorts the
  pawn ([[positional/pawn-strengths]], [[endgames/king-pawn-endings]]).
- **Rooks belong BEHIND passed pawns** — yours (to push) and theirs (to stop).
- **Take the opposition** in king-and-pawn endings (kings facing with one square
  between → the side NOT to move is squeezed). See [[endgames/king-pawn-endings]].
- **Know your mates:** with K+Q or K+R vs a lone king you can force mate — drive
  the king to the edge ([[mates/index]]). Keep the piece that mates.

## Don't
- **Don't leave your king passive** — an active enemy king will outplay you.
- **Don't trade your last piece that can force mate** for a tiny material gain
  ([[principles/material-and-trading]]).
- **Don't stalemate** a lone king when you're winning — give it a square, check
  before every quiet move when it has few moves ([[principles/avoid-stalemate]]).
- **Don't rush passed pawns into a blockade** without king/rook support.

## Convert cleanly
- If you're winning, **simplify toward a known win** — but verify the resulting
  endgame is actually winning before trading down (K+minor vs K is often a draw).
- Trade pieces, not pawns, when ahead; you need pawns to promote.
- Make a concrete plan: name the pawn, the file, the route
  ([[strategy/convert-advantage]]).

## Every move still applies
Run the checklist ([[fundamentals/every-move-checklist]]) — even in a "simple"
endgame, watch for stalemate tricks, a sudden enemy passed pawn, and back-rank
ideas.

**One line:** *king up, passed pawns forward (rook behind them), keep your mating
piece, and don't stalemate the win.*
